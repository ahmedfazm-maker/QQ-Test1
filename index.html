Introduction

What this report is, how we produced it, and how to use it.
This is an independent assessment of Meshwar, produced from the 14 documents in your data room, your answers to six follow-up questions, and the profile you completed at onboarding.
We did three things, in order. First we read and analysed everything — extracting facts, recomputing your key metrics from source data rather than accepting stated figures, and checking your documents against each other. Then we asked about what the documents could not resolve on their own. Finally we scored the venture against a 96-metric rubric.
Two things worth knowing before you read on. Where we had no data we recorded a gap rather than an estimate — nothing here is invented to fill a hole. And where your documents disagreed with each other we say so plainly; in almost every case that turns out to be a definition difference rather than an error, and we've said which we think it is.
Sections 03–09 are the analysis. Sections 10–12 are the assessment. Sections 13–15 are what to do next. If you read only two things, read the executive summary and section 14.

How your score works

Worth two minutes before the numbers — the rubric changes depending on your stage, and the score means different things at different confidence levels.
The rubric is dynamic. A venture is only measured on what's measurable at its stage. At Stage 1 we don't ask about churn; at Stage 6 we do. Of 96 metrics in the full rubric, 54 apply to a Stage 6 business — the other 42 were excluded as not applicable, not counted as zero.
The weights shift too. Early on, the team and the market matter most — there's little else to measure. As revenue arrives, Viability dominates, because the question changes from "could this work" to "does this work."

03

Business profile

What we understood your business to be — reconstructed from your documents rather than your deck. Worth checking we got it right.
Meshwar sells a fleet dispatch and proof-of-delivery platform to FMCG distributors in Greater Cairo and Alexandria. Revenue is a per-vehicle monthly subscription (EGP 340–520) plus a one-off onboarding fee, billed quarterly in advance.
Nineteen paying distributors across 1,140 tracked vehicles. Recognised revenue of EGP 5.9M in FY24, growing 31% year on year. Eleven staff on payroll, two further contributors invoicing as contractors. Incorporated in Egypt as a single LLC.
One thing worth flagging
Your deck leads on AI route optimisation. Your analytics show route optimisation used by 3 of 19 accounts. Proof-of-delivery is used daily by all 19 and is barely mentioned in your materials. The business you're selling and the business you're pitching aren't the same one — and the one you're selling looks like the stronger story.
